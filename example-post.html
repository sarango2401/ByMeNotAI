How transistors went from room-sized to invisible

April 2025

In 1947, three physicists at Bell Labs invented a device the size of a human palm that would eventually shrink to a width of a few nanometers — smaller than a strand of DNA. The transistor is arguably the most consequential invention of the twentieth century, and most people have never thought about how it works.

This is my attempt to explain it — starting from semiconductor physics, ending with modern chip manufacturing, and stopping at all the interesting parts along the way.

What a transistor actually does

At its core, a transistor is a switch controlled by electricity. Apply a small voltage to the right place, and current flows through; remove it, and current stops. That is it. The magic is not in what it does — it is in how small and fast it can be made to do it.
